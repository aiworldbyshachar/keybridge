Privacy

Last updated November 9, 2026

In short:

  • KeyBridge never records what you type. It only learns that one of your shortcuts was pressed.
  • Without an account, nothing leaves your computer.
  • With an account, your shortcuts are locked on your computer with a code only you have before they're sent. The server can't read them, and neither can I.
  • No ads, no tracking, no analytics, in the app or on this site.

What KeyBridge does with your keyboard

To notice your shortcuts, a small helper inside KeyBridge watches the keyboard (a keyboard hook on Windows, an event tap on the Mac - the same thing every hotkey app uses).

  • It compares each key combination with your shortcuts and tells the app only which shortcut was pressed, never which keys you typed.
  • For abbreviations (like ;sig + Space) it remembers the last few keys you pressed, in memory only. They're never written to disk and never sent anywhere, and they're forgotten as you go on typing.
  • When a shortcut types long text, KeyBridge puts it on the clipboard for a moment, pastes it, and puts back what you had there. That moment is marked so clipboard managers skip it. A shortcut that uses {clipboard} reads the clipboard when you press it, and only then.

What stays on your computer

In KeyBridge's data folder (%APPDATA%\KeyBridge on Windows, ~/Library/Application Support/KeyBridge on the Mac):

Your shortcuts, groups and trashshortcuts.json, including how often you used each shortcut. Usage counts never leave the computer.
Settingssettings.json: language, open at login, this computer's name.
Your sign-in and codesecrets.json, a file only your user can read. On Windows it's also sealed with the system's secure storage.
Sync notessync.json: what this computer last agreed with the server, so only changes are sent.
Logkeybridge.log: when KeyBridge started, how many shortcuts were synced, errors. Never what you type, what your shortcuts say, or your email address.

If you export your shortcuts to a file, that file is readable as text - keep it somewhere private.

What's stored in the cloud - only if you sign in

Signing in is optional. When you do, KeyBridge keeps a copy of your shortcuts in a database hosted by Supabase, so your other computers can get them. Before anything is sent, it's locked on your computer with a key made from your code (AES-256-GCM). The code itself never leaves your computers.

Your accountYour email address and when you signed in. The email is used to send you sign-in links and for nothing else - no newsletters, no marketing.
Your shortcuts, groups and trashLocked with your code - unreadable without it. Stored next to each one: a random id, a revision number, whether it was deleted, which of your computers changed it, and when.
A check valueA short value locked with your code, so KeyBridge can say "wrong code" when one is mistyped.
Your computersFor each one: a random id, its name (locked with your code too), whether it's a Mac or a PC, and when it was last seen - so you can see your computers and sign one out.

Each account can only reach its own rows. As the operator I can see that an account exists, its email address, how many items it holds and when they changed - but not what they say. Supabase, which hosts the database and handles sign-in, keeps the usual technical records of sign-ins and requests (such as times and IP addresses) for security, for a limited time.

How long it's kept, and how to remove it

  • Items in the Trash are removed for good after 30 days, on every computer and in the cloud.
  • Everything else in the cloud is kept while you have an account.
  • Account → Sign out or delete → Delete account removes your account and everything stored with it from the database right away. Your shortcuts stay on your computers.
  • Account → Your computers → Sign out signs out a computer you no longer have.
  • Settings → Export to a file gives you all your shortcuts as a file you can read.
  • Can't reach the app any more? Write to aiworldbyshachar@gmail.com from the account's email address and I'll delete the account for you.

If you lose your code

Any computer that's still signed in shows it again (Account → Show my code). Nobody else has it, so it can't be reset: if the code and every signed-in computer are lost, the locked copy in the cloud can't be opened by anyone, me included.
