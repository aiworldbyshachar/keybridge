Right and left are different keys

Record the keys by pressing them, and KeyBridge shows exactly which side you used. So Right Shift + A can type your address while Left Shift + A is still just a capital A.

  • Shift, Ctrl, Alt and Win / Cmd, each side on its own - or either side.
  • Or an abbreviation: type ;sig and Space, and it's your signature. It fires on the English and the Hebrew layout alike.
  • Type text, open a link, a folder or a file, or start a program. Text can hold the date, the time, the clipboard, and where the cursor ends up.
  • Long text is pasted, and your clipboard comes back right after.

The same shortcuts on your Mac

Sign in on your Mac and your PC and they share every shortcut. Keys are matched by what they do: Right Alt on Windows is Right Option on the Mac, Right Win is Right Cmd.

  • Right Cmd + C can type text while Left Cmd + C still copies.
  • Most Mac keyboards have no Right Control - KeyBridge tells you, and suggests a key that's free.
  • A shortcut can work on all your computers, or only on Windows or only on the Mac.

Only you can read them

Your shortcuts are locked on your computer with a code only you have, before they go anywhere. The server keeps them locked - not even I can read them.

  • KeyBridge never records what you type. It only knows when one of your shortcuts was pressed.
  • See all your computers, and sign out one you no longer have.
  • No account? Then nothing leaves your computer. What's stored where

Nothing is ever lost

Deleted shortcuts, and versions replaced by an import or by another computer, wait in the Trash for 30 days. One click restores them on every computer.

  • Changed the same shortcut on two computers before they could sync? The later change wins, and the other one is kept as “Older version”.

Bring your shortcuts along

Coming from Clavier+ on Windows? KeyBridge finds your shortcuts and brings them in with their left and right keys. Export everything to a file whenever you like.

  • Opens at login and lives quietly in the tray or the menu bar.
  • Pause all shortcuts with one click - for games or remote desktop.

Speaks your language

English, עברית, Español, Français, Русский and العربية - with full right-to-left layouts for Hebrew and Arabic. Your text is typed exactly as you wrote it, in any language.

Install

KeyBridge isn't signed with paid Apple/Microsoft certificates, so both systems ask once before opening it.

Windows

  1. Run KeyBridge-Windows-Setup.exe.
  2. If you see “Windows protected your PC”, click More info → Run anyway.
  3. KeyBridge opens, and from then on starts quietly with Windows.

Mac

  1. Open KeyBridge-macOS.dmg and drag KeyBridge into Applications.
  2. Open it once. If macOS says it can't verify it, go to System Settings → Privacy & Security and click Open Anyway.
  3. Allow KeyBridge under Privacy & Security → Accessibility - the window has a button that takes you there. That's what lets it see your shortcuts.
If macOS says the app “is damaged”, run this once in Terminal:xattr -cr /Applications/KeyBridge.app

Questions

Does KeyBridge see what I type?

No. A small helper watches for your shortcuts and tells the app only that one of them was pressed. For abbreviations it keeps the last few keys in memory, to compare them - never on disk, never sent anywhere. The log never contains what you type.

Do I need an account?

No. Without one, KeyBridge works fully and everything stays on your computer. The account only adds your shortcuts on your other computers.

What's stored in the cloud?

Your email address, your shortcuts locked with your code, and a short list of your computers (their names locked too). Nobody can read the shortcuts without your code - not even me. Delete your account in the app and it's all gone. The privacy page has every detail.

What if I lose my code?

Any computer that's still signed in shows it again: Account → Show my code. Since nobody else has it, it can't be reset - so write it down when you first see it. An export to a file is a good extra backup.

Does it work in every app?

In nearly all of them: browsers, Word, Notepad, TextEdit, terminals. Two exceptions: on Windows, apps run as administrator (like Task Manager) are kept apart from normal apps; on the Mac, password fields block every app that watches the keyboard, on purpose.

Will it get in the way of normal typing?

Only the exact keys you choose are taken: Right Shift + A can be a shortcut while Left Shift + A still types A. KeyBridge warns you when keys are used by the system or by another shortcut, and one click pauses everything for games or remote desktop.

I use Clavier+.

KeyBridge offers to bring your shortcuts in the first time it starts, and later in Settings → Import from Clavier+. They keep their left and right keys. Anything it can't do yet comes in switched off, with the reason.

Is it really free?

Yes. KeyBridge is open source under the MIT licence. No ads, no tracking.
