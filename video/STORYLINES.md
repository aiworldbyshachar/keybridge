# KeyBridge demo film - storylines

Same family film as ClipBridge and FolderBridge: about 86 s (ClipBridge's length; FolderBridge's 98 s
was a bit long), 1920x1080, the warm desk (MacBook Air left, 27" PC monitor right), George's voice, the
accent bridge as the signature visual, the real app UI with the same made-up data as the website
(Dana Levi, example.com). One object follows the whole film. Shape: hook -> the old way (3 detours) ->
"what if..." + reveal -> core action on both computers -> sign in + the one rule -> depth -> privacy ->
six languages -> payoff -> end card.

Different from the two earlier films: KeyBridge's bridge goes through your account, not the Wi-Fi.
So the one rule is "sign in on each computer", and the privacy beat is "locked with a code only you
have" - never "no cloud". No competitor is named or shown (Clavier+ stays out of the film).

**Picked (2026-11-09): A, the email address.**

## A. The email address (recommended, picked)

**Object:** one line of text, `dana.levi@example.com`, typed again and again.

| Time | Beat | On screen | Voice (draft) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0-12 s | Hook | The desk. On the PC a web form's Email field, the address typed letter by letter (soft key ticks). Then on the Mac a reply in Mail - the same address, letter by letter, again. | "Two computers. One desk. A Mac... and a PC." / "And one email address you type twenty times a day. In forms. In replies. Letter... by letter." |
| 12-20 s | The old way | Desaturated, a cut every ~2.3 s: a notes file "things I paste" - find, copy, switch, paste; typing it again ends as `dana.levi@exmaple.com` with a red underline; a new laptop opened - nothing set up. | "[sighs] So you keep it in a note and copy it from there. Type it again... and get one letter wrong. And on a new computer, you start all over." |
| 20-26 s | Reveal | The accent bridge draws between the screens carrying one white keycap, "Right Shift". The icon blooms, the wordmark wipes in, the music opens. | "What if one key could just... type it for you? On every computer?" / "This is KeyBridge." |
| 26-36 s | Core: left and right | Big keycaps: Right Shift + E - the whole address lands in the form at once. Left Shift + E - just "E". Then the real editor with Right Shift + E recorded. | "Right Shift and E... and there it is. Left Shift and E? Still just a capital E. In KeyBridge, left and right are different keys." |
| 36-46 s | Sign in + the one rule | On the Mac: the real Sign in card, the email, then the code box with its XXXX-XXXX-XXXX template filling in; the list fills with every shortcut, Right Ctrl shown as Right Cmd. The rule line card. | "On your Mac, sign in and type your code once. Every shortcut is already there. The only rule: sign in on each computer." |
| 46-60 s | Depth | `;sig` + Space - a three-line signature in the Mail reply. Right Alt + C - the team calendar opens. The real list: groups, search "addr", a delete, then Trash and Restore. | "Type a short word, and get your whole signature. Open a link, a folder or a program with one key. Group them, search them... and nothing you delete is ever lost." |
| 60-69 s | Privacy | A shortcut card crosses the bridge and turns to scrambled text with a lock on it. A keyboard with only "Email - pressed" coming out of it. One click on Pause. | "Your shortcuts are locked with a code only you have, before they leave your computer. Nobody else can read them... not even us. And KeyBridge never records what you type." |
| 69-74 s | Six languages | The real UI switching language on each spoken name, right to left for Hebrew and Arabic. | "In English, Hebrew, Spanish, French, Russian, or Arabic." |
| 74-80 s | Payoff | Back to the Mail reply at normal speed: address, link, signature - a whole reply in three keystrokes, then Send. | "So stop typing the same things over and over. Just press it... once." |
| 80-86 s | End card | Icon + wordmark on #f5f5f8, "Free & open source · Mac & Windows", "Download free" + the URL, held 3 s. | "KeyBridge. Free and open source, for Mac and Windows." |

Why: it's the pain everyone has every day, the left/right trick is the core beat (it's what makes
KeyBridge different), and the account comes in as the second surprise - "and it's on your Mac too".
The payoff is the same email as the hook, finished in seconds.

## B. The new laptop

**Object:** a brand-new MacBook out of the box, next to the PC.

- **Hook:** "A new laptop. Fast, clean... and it knows nothing about you." Years of shortcuts live on the PC.
- **The old way:** a settings file emailed to yourself; "not available for macOS"; retyping shortcuts one by one at night.
- **Reveal**, then **core**: sign in on the Mac, type the code - every shortcut flows over the bridge into the list, keys translated as they cross (Right Win becomes Right Cmd).
- **Then** the left/right beat, depth, privacy, languages as in A.
- **Payoff:** the first email on the new laptop, typed with the same keys as always.

Why: puts the account - the new part of this app - at the centre. But the daily pain (typing the
same things) comes second, and a new computer is a rare moment, so the hook is less "that's me".

## C. Two Shift keys

**Object:** the keyboard itself, close up on its two Shift keys.

- **Hook:** "Your keyboard has two Shift keys. Two Ctrls. Two Alts. And they do exactly the same thing."
- **The old way:** a hotkey that steals Ctrl+C from copy; a cheat sheet taped to the monitor; a shortcut that fires while you're just typing a capital letter.
- **Reveal:** the bridge joins a left key to a right key.
- **Core:** the right-hand keys type your things, the left ones stay normal; Right Cmd + C types text while Left Cmd + C still copies.
- **Then** sign in on the other computer, depth, privacy, languages.
- **Payoff:** a whole morning's emails typed with the right-hand keys.

Why: the most distinctive and visual (big keycaps all the way through), made for keyboard people.
Colder than A, and the "on every computer" part gets squeezed.
